Speaker: Andrew Hodges (University of Oxford)

Title: Alan Turing, The Absent Father of Computing

Abstract:

Alan Turing (1912-1954) has often been dubbed the 'Father' of
computing, this vague word conveying the complex connection between On
Computable Numbers in 1936, the digital computing plans of 1945, the
first working electronic computers in 1948-51, and computer science
thereafter. I will address this complexity, starting with an update on
the well-known question of whether and how von Neumann’s EDVAC report
of 1945 was related to Turing’s work. But my emphasis will be on what
happened after 1945, regarding it as a puzzle that Turing drew so
little attention to his own 1936 foundations, despite their relevance
to his famous Intelligent Machinery predictions. His extraordinary
reticence affected the emergent science and technology of computing,
as I will illustrate with some little-known documents. My conclusion
is summarised in the title: it is fair to consider him a ‘father’, but
he avoided taking either praise or responsibility for his offspring.

About the speaker:

Andrew Hodges was for many years a tutor and lecturer in mathematics
at Oxford University. His research was mainly as a colleague of Roger
Penrose in making a new approach to mathematical physics, and in this
field he was also a visitor to the Institute for Advanced Study,
Princeton, working with Nima Arkani-Hamed. He is now an Emeritus
Fellow of Wadham College, Oxford. He is best known as the author of
Alan Turing: the Enigma, published in 1983. In the forty years since
then he has contributed further to the world of Turing studies, with a
website at turing.org.uk. In an unusual career he has also seen
various adaptations of this work in the arts and media, most recently
in collaborating with the Pet Shop Boys in A man from the future, a
musical piece released in October 2026.
