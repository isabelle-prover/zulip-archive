I'm happy to announce a very rare contribution involving non-standard analysis. It's great to see NSA applied to justify “unsound" historical mathematics.

Euler's Exponential Series as an Infinite Polynomial
Jacques D. Fleuriot

In this formalisation, we reconstruct Euler's derivation of the power series for the exponential function as expounded in his famous Introductio in analysin infinitorum, first published in 1748. Using nonstandard analysis, we mechanize his mixture of infinitesimal and infinite `algebraic' reasoning in the proof assistant Isabelle. In so doing, we demonstrate that the gist of his arguments can be reconstructed formally, with Isabelle and nonstandard analysis shoring up crucial aspects of his reasoning that some historians have qualified as being ``more a matter of faith than science''.

https://isa-afp.org/entries/Euler_Exponential.html

Algebraic Invariants of the Circle, Torus, and Punctured Plane
Arthur Freitas Ramos, David Barros Hulak and Ruy Jose Guerra Barretto de Queiroz

We formalize real path lifts of based loops in the circle and prove that their integer endpoints classify these loops up to path homotopy. For the torus, the two coordinate lifts give a classification by pairs of integers and an explicit fundamental-group isomorphism with . Every continuous circle self-map acts on first singular homology by multiplication by its winding number. We construct a radial deformation retraction of the punctured complex plane onto the circle and compute the reduced homology of both spaces: it is isomorphic to the additive group of integers in degree one and trivial in every other degree. Neither space is contractible. The development also provides a reusable interface for path lifting in covering spaces.

https://isa-afp.org/entries/Circle_Torus_Algebraic_Topology.html
